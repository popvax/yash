# Unresolved safety and development questions

The computational checks establish specified sequence properties and reproduce recorded model results. They do not establish clinical safety, tumour recognition or benefit. Lower predicted junction-binding counts cannot be translated into a probability of safety or response.

## Target evidence

Most tumour evidence comes from the 15 January 2025 liver biopsy (`TN25-112103`, ~60–70% histological tumour cellularity alongside liver parenchyma/stroma; histological cellularity does not measure the RNA mixture fraction). Longitudinal Guardant360 plasma testing on 14 October 2025 reported 31.9% epigenomic ctDNA tumour fraction (up from 1.4% on 29 March 2025, a 22.8× increase) alongside project-narrative progression in lung and peritoneum while liver and pancreatic lesions were described as controlled (`data/clinical-timeline.md`): seven panel-covered somatic calls from the January biopsy were also detected in October 2025 cfDNA (`CDH1::NRG1`, `TERT c.-124C>T`, `ROS1 F1699F`, `MERTK E192*` [0 ALT / 25 REF liver WTS fragments, compatible with NMD or absent mutant transcription rather than proof of NMD], `JUN R276*` [stop-gain creating no novel sequence], `RXRA A214S`, `TGFBR2 N515Kfs*26`), alongside `KMT2D p.R2789Q` (`c.8366G>A`, `0.2% -> 3.2%` [16.0×], cancer-cell fraction and lesion origin unestablished; `0/139` liver WTS ALT fragments; `AlphaMissense = 0.1775` and unverified reference-model splice scores), low-VAF `AR P555S` (0.4%), `DHX36 L147L` (0.3%), `ERBB4 D113V` (0.3%), `DHX36 R160K` (0.2%) and `RAF1 G535S` (0.2%), plus `DNMT3A C351Afs*56` (`0.2% -> 0.1%`, annotated by Guardant360 as potential clonal hematopoiesis [potential CHIP] and excluded from targeting). Thirteen of Design 4's 15 somatic mutation genes are absent from Guardant360's targeted panel, so their persistence in October 2025 cfDNA and progressing lesions is unmeasured, and Guardant360 reports no `B2M` SNV/indel/methylation alteration (`B2M^`) while not marking `B2M` (`15q21.1`) for copy-number amplification (`†`) or deletion (`*`) or reporting somatic `HLA-A/B/C` LOH (`review/clinical-reconciliation.json`, `review/missing-data-priorities.tsv`). The later matched-normal sample (`2026-10-30`) supports germline DNA exclusion but does not establish current tumour expression. Somatic allele fractions and pooled cfDNA VAFs do not determine clonality or anatomical lesion origin without accounting for purity, copy number, mutant-copy multiplicity and shedding heterogeneity.

KCTD19 has no coding-strand mutant RNA fragments in the audited sample (`0` coding / `2` opposite-strand; reference AlphaGenome 2 tracks show higher predicted activity in reference testis than in reference pancreas/liver/lung, which does not establish absence of tumour transcription). FES and SYT12 each have one coding-strand mutant fragment. Shallow sampling leaves expression uncertain. The KCTD19-excluded 15-region construct (`strand-strict-15/`, including its 481-aa protein, 1,586-nt known RNA segment, fresh junction/presentation recheck and full `strand-strict-15/safety/` screens) is a computational alternative; it has no independent evidence of clinical performance. KCTD19 is not an active expression question for that excluded panel; its other target, HLA and presentation uncertainties remain. The clinically reported CDH1::NRG1 fusion does not establish presentation of the proposed junction peptide. Its [source support](exports/inframe-selection.json) records 200 junction reads and one spanning fragment in distinct, non-UMI units; these are not 201 independently counted molecules. Guardant360 also reports `UGT1A1 *1/*28` and `CYP2D6 *4/*41` intermediate-metabolizer pharmacogenomics.

## Presentation and specificity

Caris reports predicted Class-I genotypes from tumour DNA and recommends confirmation with an appropriate assay; it cautions that tumour sequencing does not replace blood-based histocompatibility testing. Class-II typing here is a research inference from tumour DNA/RNA. Its chain pairing is unphased, the evaluated combinations are incomplete, and the DRB3 calls disagree. The engineered protein's processing, trafficking and peptide presentation remain unmeasured.

Exact-reference searches cover the supplied coding references. They omit incomplete patient-normal models and unmeasured noncanonical proteins and modifications. Related normal peptides, including KLF-family, histone H2B and RXR-family homologs, warrant investigation. Sequence similarity and predicted binding identify candidates for cross-recognition; they do not demonstrate toxicity.

The exact-match exclusion applies to nominated `TARGET` queries, which have already passed exact-reference filtering. Junction matches are recorded separately and are not automatically excluded by that target-selection rule. In particular, the alternative's `STVMAPNLF` and the main construct's `GAGGGRGD` have exact reference matches. Eleven hypothetical UTR-frame peptides also match alternative HBA1 translations in GENCODE v50, but not the v46 translations. These findings and their processing limits are recorded in [the reference-match notes](review/reference-match-findings.md); none demonstrates presentation, autoimmunity or absence of harm.

The [screening policy](safety/safety-summary.json) distinguishes broad query admission (including PRIME rank ≤2%) from downstream homolog classification (PRIME rank ≤0.5%). The shared-HLA predicate requires each peptide on the same allele to satisfy MHCflurry nM ≤500 **or** MHCflurry percentile ≤2 **or** MixMHCpred rank ≤2; PRIME is not part of that predicate. Different stage cutoffs are declared analytical choices, not calibrated clinical risk boundaries.

The 197 Class-I controls (`safety/wetlab-negative-control-peptides.tsv` for the 16-region construct; `184` in `strand-strict-15/safety/wetlab-negative-control-peptides.tsv` for the 15-region alternative) cover 100% of shared-HLA own-wild-type Class-I counterparts and 1-mismatch same-HLA paralogs under the stated thresholds, plus prioritized 2–4 mismatch homologs. The 39 Class-II controls (`37` in `strand-strict-15/safety/wetlab-class2-wt-controls.tsv`) are positional wild-type 15-mers. Both constructs also independently screen every stop-delimited sense (`1,915` and `1,782` 8–11-mers; `36` and `30` with MHCflurry percentile `<=0.5%` on their minimum-nM allele, or `38` and `32` with `<=0.5%` on any of Yash's six Class-I alleles) and antisense cryptic-ORF peptide (`safety/cryptic-orf-peptides.tsv`, `strand-strict-15/safety/cryptic-orf-peptides.tsv`). Testing these panels would have assay-specific detection limits and would not exclude every possible off-target response.

Antisense counts describe hypothetical reverse-complement IVT-byproduct sequences, not observed product impurities or expressed antigens. Their biological relevance requires the corresponding RNA species and its translation; neither is measured here. Predicted binding of those hypothetical peptides is not a toxicity rate.

## RNA and product definition

The longest uninterrupted stem in one predicted minimum-energy RNA structure is 11 paired bases. This is a model result, not a maximum over all conformations or a validated PKR safety threshold. Experiments have demonstrated PKR activation by some short stem-loop RNAs with single-stranded arms ([Mayo et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27208315/)); this does not show that Design 4 activates PKR.

The motif, alternative-frame-stop and folding checks do not measure expression, alternative translation, RNA stability, manufacturing impurities or innate immune activation. Cap chemistry, nucleotide modifications, PolyA and delivery are unspecified. Product-specific specifications, assays and acceptance criteria require qualified development work.

Restriction-site and `AATAAA` occurrences are sequence facts, not demonstrated manufacturing failures. Their relevance depends on the cloning/linearisation method and on whether expression uses T7 IVT RNA or a nuclear DNA-expression system. No codon edits are proposed here. A motif substitution is not necessarily synonymous when it crosses codon boundaries. Cysteine counts and N-X-S/T sequons likewise do not establish free thiols, aggregation, glycosylation occupancy or altered presentation.

The [research questions](safety/research-questions.tsv) identify unresolved work, not validated release standards. The 30-bp MFE reporting cutoff in the software is an author-selected comparison, not an experimentally validated activation boundary. No dose, product-release thresholds or clinical route are specified.

Any clinical investigation would require a treating team, sponsor, ethical review and the applicable regulatory authorization. FDA individual-patient expanded access is one conditional US pathway ([FDA guidance](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/expanded-access/expanded-access-information-physicians)); this analysis does not determine eligibility or select a route. This research should not delay established oncology care.

The files retaining the legacy name `wetlab-negative-control-peptides.tsv` contain **candidate specificity-comparison peptides**, not experimentally established negative controls. Class-II `best_context_rank_pct` and `best_no_context_rank_pct` annotate predictions for the mutant peptide with and without flanking context; they are not mutant-versus-WT comparisons or predictions for the paired WT peptide. Each construct uses its own assembled context.
