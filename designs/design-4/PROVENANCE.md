# Data attribution and identity checks

The source project attributes case `TN25-112103` to Yash. The supplied Caris report names Yashdeep Bindal, identifies that case and records liver tissue collected on 15 January 2025. The RNA alignment uses the case label. The matched-normal label `TN25-112103-N` was assigned during analysis; that label is not independent identity authentication.

The repository's recorded tumour–normal DNA fingerprint test reports a positive LOD of **48.887978**, with 933 markers in 240 haplotype blocks. This supports donor concordance under that test. It is a recorded source result, not a newly repeated whole-DNA fingerprint run.

An independent check compared the available RNA alignment with the supplied normal coding-genotype data at **110 common SNP markers**, five per autosome and at least 1 Mb apart. Selection used coverage and quality, without choosing markers for agreement. It found 93 exact threshold-genotype matches, ten imbalanced/uncertain RNA calls and seven normal heterozygotes called homozygous in RNA. All eight normal homozygous-alternate markers had matching RNA calls, with no opposite-homozygote conflicts at those eight markers. The causes of the other seventeen nonmatching or uncertain RNA calls are unresolved; no claim is made that all 110 markers match. The counts support concordance; they are not a calibrated identity probability or a Picard LOD.

The checks link the supplied files and support a shared donor. They do not authenticate specimen collection, lab custody or the named person's identity. They also do not establish that an old biopsy represents every current lesion. A clinical attribution would require authenticated laboratory/clinical records and custody evidence.

The complete RNA BAM (`1,317,202,830` bytes per the repository file catalog [`data/files.tsv`](../../data/files.tsv), whose recorded SHA-256 for that file matches `RNA_BAM_sha256` in [`review/identity-concordance.json`](review/identity-concordance.json)) and relevant downloaded inputs were content-checked in the recorded identity-concordance run, while offline verification ([`verify_results.py`](verify_results.py)) hash-checks all bundled artifacts in `designs/design-4/` (and external GENCODE translation FASTAs when `--rescan-reference` is used) without re-reading the raw RNA BAM. The approximately 143.29 GB normal and 13.26 GB tumour DNA BAMs were available for indexed queries; their complete payload checksums were not independently verified. A checksum establishes file identity, not the truth of a specimen label.

See [the verification receipt](review/verification.json) for the scope of computational checks. No claim of authenticated patient identity, safety or benefit follows from these results.

The Guardant RXRA label A214S and the selected A241S peptide use different isoforms. The local WT window occurs at A214 in ENST00000672570 and A241 in ENST00000481739.2. For the versioned RefSeq NM_002957.6, the correct coding citation is c.721G>T (GCC to TCC, A241S). The previous c.640G>T citation was incorrect for that accession: it would encode D214Y. The reporting-label correction changes no genomic allele, read count, peptide or predicted binding value. The ctDNA receipts distinguish this annotation edit from their original raw-read/model execution.

For DNMT3A, Guardant prints C351Afs*56. The supplied genomic deletion maps to NM_022552.5:c.1048_1051del, whose reference CDS and independent Ensembl annotation predict p.Phe350AlafsTer56. The report label and transcript-specific prediction are now recorded separately; neither is silently substituted for the other. DNMT3A remains excluded from targeting.
